# KIT-SPEC

This paper is the list of every choice the spec leaves open, derived from
the spec alone. Quo is what crosses the door, chapter 3, and what the
stance and the ground mean, chapters 4 and 5. Chapter 6 is the one carrier
Quo publishes, and the door stands without it. Everything else the spec
hands to the kit, the being, the harbor or the carrier on purpose, and says
once what the choice costs. A kit is one set of those choices. Two kits
choosing differently are both Quo, and that is the proof Quo is a protocol.
Here each choice is a question, so that an author answers all of them
before writing a line, and a reader of a kit finds each one answered in
that kit's own `KIT-SPEC.md`, which is this list with every question
answered and the reason given.

## From chapter 3, the door

1. **Which base64 do bytes travel as?** "Bytes travel as base64 strings,
   and which base64 is the two beings' business, since no ward decodes
   one." No cost is named, because no ward reads one.

2. **Does this ward stand a public being, and which being is she?** "A ward
   may have one public being and no more." The cost of standing none is
   named: "A ward without a public being answers arrivals for nobody with
   silence."

3. **How long does a door keep the keys of a removed heir?** "How long a
   door keeps those keys is the ward's own." The cost: "the holder of a key
   past it hears silence where she would have heard `removed`, which is
   what every stranger hears and tells her nothing she can act on."

4. **What is a ward's default allowance and what is its ceiling?** "How
   wide a ward's default and ceiling are is that ward's own, and no far
   door can tell one ward's from another's." The cost is that every wait
   must end: "The innermost wait gives up, and the ask ends."

5. **What does a kit hold beside the mark to know which numbers below it
   are spent?** "The door keeps the highest number honoured, the **mark**,
   and which numbers below it are spent, out to a span of sixty-four." The
   span is Quo's; how the sixty-four are held is not.

6. **How does a kit hash a blueprint into its digest in its own
   language?** The rule is Quo's, "SHA-256, as lowercase hex, over the JCS
   (RFC 8785) canonical serialization", but the two places named are where
   a kit must choose its own route: "Keys sort by UTF-16 code unit, not by
   code point and not by byte", and numbers "as ECMAScript writes them".
   The cost of choosing wrong: "A kit that hashed otherwise would make
   every standing between the two wards refresh on every ask."

7. **What does the kit keep for its operator when a reply is none of the
   three shapes?** "A reply ... that is none of the three shapes, is
   silence to the sender", and "an answer a far being really gave is lost
   when the kit that wrote it wrote one field wrong." The being hears
   silence and nothing else. Whether the kit records, for its own eyes and
   never on the wire, which rule the reply broke is its own.

8. **Does the door equalize the time of its refusals, and how far?** "The
   door equalizes the bytes and not the time it took to write them." A kit
   may decapsulate, try edge keys and verify a signature on every arrival
   whatever it holds, or refuse as early as each case allows. What the
   early refusal costs is said there: a holder of an invitation learns by
   timing what state its heir is in.

## From chapter 4, the stance

1. **How is the stance spelled?** "This is meaning, never a language's
   spelling. A kit spells it however its language spells things, and that
   is the whole reason it is meaning here: a stance is spelled by a kit,
   and nothing on the wire observes the spelling."

2. **How is a being handed her cells?** "**Cells.** Her state, values only.
   A write of a non-value is refused where she wrote it, in her own frame."
   The refusal's place is fixed; the shape that refuses is the kit's.

3. **How is a being handed her standings?** "**She is handed** her cells,
   her standings, and six calls. Nothing more is ever offered and nothing
   here may be missing."

4. **How are the six calls spelled, and what does each take and give?**
    Invite, remove, knock, take, ask and boot are named as meaning only.
    The one bound: "Nothing more is ever offered and nothing here may be
    missing."

5. **How are the asker's three shapes spelled?** "The asker has three
    shapes and no fourth. Her own id for the occupant at the door. Nobody,
    which is how the public being knows she is public. The root, which is
    the ward speaking to her on behalf of whoever holds its unsealed ask.
    Nothing else ever reaches her."

6. **Which ids does the kit reserve, and how does it refuse them?** "A kit
    reserves whatever names its own spelling of the stance would collide
    with, and refuses them at invite and at take, never at the write. Which
    names those are is the kit's, since no far ward can tell."

7. **What can the ward be asked by its root, under what names, in what
    shapes, and what does it refuse?** "Which asks those are, and their
    shapes, is the kit's."

8. **What are the asks named by which one ward pilots another?** "A ward
    piloted from another ward is piloted by ordinary asks on a relation,
    and what those asks are named is a matter between the two kits and not
    a promise of Quo."

9. **How may two asks be inside one being at once?** "Her cells are hers
    to guard across every point her language may leave her answer and come
    back. Her ward adds no serialisation of its own, and two asks may be
    inside her at once." The kit chooses the concurrency it offers; the
    guarding is hers.

10. **What holds the line of one relation, and what releases it?** "Every
    send on one relation waits for the one before it". The cost is named:
    "A line is released by the allowance and by nothing else: an ask that
    never comes back holds its line, and a kit that bounds where a being is
    waiting and not where the line is held has a relation that one quiet
    far side closes for good."

11. **How does a being name the class she boots, and how does she choose
    the key it is under?** "A being may make a being of her own ward, by
    class name and under a key she chooses."

12. **How does an ask between two beings of one ward travel?** The spec
    binds only the relation: the booted being holds "no relation but the
    one her maker named, and that one is made the way all of them are:
    invited, knocked and taken." Whether those bytes go through the seal
    and a carrier is the kit's.

13. **Who invites at boot, and which ids are named?** "A boot whose
    relation could not be made makes nobody." Which side mints which id,
    and what a kit calls them, follows the invite rule alone: invite
    "refuses an id that already names a record, occupant or standing, since
    the two are one namespace."

14. **How does a restart hand a being her cells again?** "a restart hands
    her the same cells again. What a restart is, she cannot tell from
    birth." The cost is that the restart must be invisible to her.

## From chapter 5, the ground

 1. **What is the harbor, as a thing a kit names?** "A **harbor** boots
    wards and carries bytes. It judges nothing and speaks no Quo." Quo says
    only what it hands down; how a kit binds one is not said.

 2. **How is a seed handed in and held?** "**The seed.** Thirty-two bytes,
    or what is hashed to them." The cost of custody is chapter 7: "Lose the
    seed, lose the ward, with no recovery inside Quo."

 3. **What shape is the partition, and is it sealed?** "The ward's state at
    one moment, as values, opaque to the harbor." The recommendation and
    its cost: "A careful harbor stores it sealed under a key derived from
    the seed, so that the seed is the one secret; that is a recommendation
    and not a rule, since nothing on the wire can tell whether it was
    followed."

 4. **How does one seed hold one run?** "Two harbors running one seed over
    one partition are two wards with one name. Runs that write apart
    diverge in silence". The cost is the whole of chapter 7's Divergence: "A
    relation, once diverged, is broken on both sides and is made again by
    invitation."

 5. **How is a being instantiated from a class name?** "A class name and a
    stance in, the being or nothing out. A name the harbor does not hold is
    nothing, and that boot makes nobody." The cost of a class that throws:
    "she is absent this run, her cells wait, and her door says `absent` to
    the keys it bound and silence to strangers."

 6. **Where does the random come from?** "A count in, that many bytes of
    entropy out. Every key a ward mints is drawn from it."

 7. **Which carriers does the kit stand beside the reference one?** "Any
    other carrier, a request, a held socket, a push, a queue, a link
    between planets, is a kit's own." The cost of the carrier's one
    promise: "A carrier that answers nothing after sending has turned a
    `late` into an `unreached`, and a being who then asks again has asked
    twice."

 8. **How does the door tell its harbor whether what was written is kept,
    and when?** "Whether what was written is kept is the one thing a ward
    may learn about storage, once, after the being has answered and before
    the reply is sealed, and it learns yes or no and never a reason." The
    cost: "A door that says yes to what was never kept is the worse fault".

 9. **How does the harbor rate a ward key by the one bit it learns?** "A
    ward key that only ever brings strangers' bytes is the harbor's to rate
    or refuse, and Quo says nothing about how."

## From chapter 6, the reference carrier

 1. **How does a dialer learn which address stands a ward?** "How a dialer
    learns which address stands a ward is outside this chapter and outside
    Quo. An invitation carries no route, so the address comes by whatever
    means the two parties already share."

 2. **Which port, and how many connections, held how long?** "A harbor that
    listens holds a TCP address, a host and a port". "A connection carries
    many asks, in flight at once, and either side may close it at any
    time." The cost of a closed line: "A connection that closes with asks
    in flight answers none of them."

 3. **How does the dialer mint frame ids?** "The **id** is minted by the
    dialer, one per ask". The one bound: "An id is unique among the asks in
    flight on one connection."

## From chapter 8, what a stranger can observe

 1. **What does the kit's conformance suite hold that the vectors cannot?**
    "whether a reply opened, which is the sender's secret; the bit beside
    the bytes, which is the door's word to its harbor; and the value of a
    partition's digest, which is the kit's own." And: "The conformance
    suite is that: a checklist written in a kit's own terms, ported by
    another kit and never shared with it."

 2. **What is the kit's partition digest, and what is the harness that
    replays a record?** "Only one thing about that digest is Quo's: two
    digests of the same ward differ when something was written between them
    and are equal when nothing was." And: "The stream it fixes, and how a
    kit is put in a record's state, are documented beside the vectors and
    are the harness, not the protocol."

## What is not a choice

- **The six algorithms.** "Ed25519 signs, X25519 agrees, ML-KEM-768
  encapsulates, SHA-256 hashes, AES-256-GCM encrypts, and HKDF-SHA-256
  derives", "named once and never negotiated", under six labels, with "the
  zero-length salt of RFC 5869 everywhere". ML-KEM-768 is taken through its
  deterministic internal forms, so the lock and every `m` come from the
  ward's random and the vectors pin them.
- **Where the lock lives.** "It is born once and lives in the partition",
  drawn "at the ward's first invite that names a heir". How the partition
  holds it is the kit's; that it is drawn there, once, is not.
- **The size.** "A door refuses the bytes of an ask whose count is above
  1,048,576". "The number is one and it is Quo's and not a ward's".
- **The depth.** "A value nests no deeper than sixty-four." "The depth bound
  is Quo's and not a ward's because a far ward reads what a near one wrote".
- **The span.** "The width is Quo's and not a ward's because a caller cannot
  tell two doors apart by anything but their answers".
- **The payload's fields.** "payload = JSON { to, by, next, seq, time,
  method?, args? }". "A field the payload owes and does not name is
  malformed."
- **The reply's shapes.** "reply = JSON { object, seen } | { silence: true }
  | { quo: word }". "A reply with a field beside its shape's ... is none of
  the three shapes."
- **The words.** "The word is one of five: `removed`, `absent`,
  `unannounced`, `repeated`, `threw`."
- **The frame.** "frame = length (4, big-endian) || body", "body = kind (1)
  || id (4, big-endian) || rest", with three kinds and a largest body of
  1,048,645 bytes.
- **The thirteen cases and their order.** "The door judges thirteen cases,
  in this order, and the first case met is the answer."
- **The digest.** "Two wards in two languages hash one blueprint to one
  digest, and a ward that cannot do this is not a ward."

## Where a kit is most easily wrong

Quo has few concepts and exact bytes. These are the sentences a careful
kit still reads wrong, each with what catches it.

- **The signature is over the bytes as received.** "A kit that
  canonicalises before verifying refuses its own peers." `framing.json`,
  the signed bodies; `SCENARIOS.md` 6.16.
- **Ed25519 refuses in four places and no others.** A torsioned key and a
  small-order `R` verify. `arithmetic.json`.
- **A number is judged on its text.** `1`, `1.0` and `1e0` are one whole
  number, `9007199254740993` and `-0` are refused, `1e21` stands. Section 6
  of `SCENARIOS.md`, and chapter 3's value rules for `1e0`.
- **Depth is counted from the value written.** An arg of sixty-four, a
  payload of sixty-six, a reply of sixty-five. The D2 records of
  `door.json` for the arg and the payload, and `framing.json` for the
  reply.
- **JCS parts languages in two places.** Keys sort by UTF-16 code unit;
  numbers are written as ECMAScript writes them. `framing.json`, the
  digests.
- **HKDF is the label's ASCII bytes and the zero-length salt.** Forty-four
  bytes out are the key then the nonce. `arithmetic.json`, `framing.json`.
- **The sender moves on an object and on nothing else.** Her signing key and
  her edge key both stay on silence, a word, `late` and `unreached`, and the
  next edge key follows the one she sent under. `SCENARIOS.md` 4.7.
- **A stray field is refused beside `ward` alone.** A ward pk with a secret
  or a lock is no invitation; a field beside the four is ignored.
  `framing.json`, the invitation.
- **Whitespace JSON allows is read everywhere.** `SCENARIOS.md` 6.18.
- **Draws happen in one order.** The lock before the heir secret, a knock's
  ephemeral secret before `m`, the reply's ephemeral key last. `door.json`,
  whose replies a kit drawing otherwise does not reproduce, and `HARNESS.md`
  section 6.

A kit that reproduces the vectors and meets another kit at the door and on
the frames is Quo, and nothing else is asked of it. This list is what a kit
says about itself, so that its choices are read as choices.
